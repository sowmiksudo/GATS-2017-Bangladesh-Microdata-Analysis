The "Missing Data" Phenomenon

The 67% missingness is driven by Structural Skip Logic, not data loss:

  • Non-smokers bypass questions about daily cigarette consumption.
  • Current smokers bypass past cessation failure timelines.
  • This ensures strict data hygiene without needing arbitrary imputation.

Feature Engineering (Pandas)

Vectorized transformations map numerical codes to analytical categories efficiently.

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# 1. Map demographic codes using dictionaries
df['Gender'] = df['A01'].map({1: 'Male', 2: 'Female'})
df['Residence'] = df['RESIDENCE'].map({1: 'Urban', 2: 'Rural'})

# 2. Bin continuous age variables
bins = [14, 24, 34, 44, 54, 64, 100]
labels = ['15-24', '25-34', '35-44', '45-54', '55-64', '65+']
df['Age_Group'] = pd.cut(df['AGE'], bins=bins, labels=labels, right=True)

# 3. Complex conditional logic for Modality Classification
conditions = [
    (df['B01'].isin([1,2])) & (df['C01'].isin([1,2])),  # Both
    (df['B01'].isin([1,2])) & (~df['C01'].isin([1,2])), # Smoke only
    (~df['B01'].isin([1,2])) & (df['C01'].isin([1,2]))  # Smokeless only
]
choices = ['Dual User', 'Smoker Only', 'Smokeless Only']
df['Tobacco_Type'] = np.select(conditions, choices, default='Non-User')

Overall Tobacco Consumption

National Prevalence

  • Smokeless dominates: More adults consume smokeless tobacco than combustible cigarettes.
  • Survey Design: Using gatsweight corrects for stratified cluster sampling.
  • Unweighted counts slightly overestimate smoking prevalence compared to the weighted national projection.

Gender Disparity: Modality Inversion

A Stark Behavioral Divide

  • Men (~40%): Overwhelmingly smoke combustible products (Cigarettes, Bidis).
  • Women (~29%): Combustible smoking is negligible, but Smokeless Tobacco (Zarda, Sadapata) usage is massive.
  • Policy Blind Spot: Traditional anti-smoking campaigns entirely miss the female demographic.

Smoking Initiation (Age Cohorts)

Youth Vulnerability

  • Median Start Age: ~18 years across all demographics.
  • Cohort Shift: Older generations had a wider spread of initiation ages.
  • Current Trend: Recent generations start almost exclusively in late adolescence, indicating critical windows for school interventions.

Age vs. Probability (Logistic Fits)

Diverging Trajectories

  • Smoking (Red): Probability peaks in middle age (30s-40s) and then levels off or declines slightly.
  • Smokeless (Blue): Probability increases linearly with age, making it highly prevalent among the elderly.
  • Confirms that smokeless tobacco is culturally entrenched in older demographics.

Socioeconomic Gradients (Education)

Education as a Protector

  • Inverse Relationship: Tobacco use drops drastically as formal education increases.
  • Respondents with "No formal education" dominate the Smokeless category.
  • University graduates strictly prefer manufactured cigarettes over smokeless, reflecting status signaling.

Daily Consumption Intensity

Cigarettes per Day

  • Volume Analysis: Median consumption hovers around 15-20 cigarettes daily.
  • Age Peak: The 45-54 age group exhibits the highest intensity of daily smoking.
  • Urban vs Rural: Urban users tend to consume slightly higher volumes than rural users.

Geographic Distribution

Urban vs. Rural

  • Rural Penetration: Smokeless tobacco use is significantly higher in rural areas.
  • Urban Shifts: Cigarette smoking holds a slightly higher relative share in urban centers.
  • Access to cheap bidis and loose Zarda heavily influences rural consumption metrics.

Secondhand Smoke (SHS) Exposure

High-Risk Environments

  • Public Transit & Dining: Despite bans, exposure remains alarmingly high in restaurants and transport.
  • Workplaces: A massive segment of the workforce is exposed to indoor SHS.
  • Homes: Deep cultural challenges in establishing smoke-free domestic rules endanger women and children.

Variable Correlation Matrix

Key Multivariate Links

  • Gender & SHS: Strong positive correlation between male gender and home SHS exposure.
  • Education: Strong negative correlation against smokeless use (-0.28).
  • Independence: Near-zero correlation between smoking and smokeless use confirms they are distinct habits.

Public Awareness Gaps

The Knowledge Deficit

  • General awareness that "smoking is harmful" is high.
  • However, specific pathological knowledge (e.g., heart attacks) drops.
  • The SHS Gap: Awareness of the severe dangers of Secondhand Smoke lags far behind active smoking, explaining poor compliance.

Healthcare & Cessation

Intervention Failures

  • Many active users attempted to quit in the past 12 months.
  • Physician Advice: Healthcare providers miss opportunities to advise quitting during routine visits.
  • Lack of formal cessation support (NRTs, counseling) results in high relapse rates.

Actionable Policy Levers

1. Taxation Harmonization

Current tax structures heavily favor Smokeless Tobacco (SLT) and bidis. Taxes must be harmonized and raised aggressively to create uniform price barriers.

2. Gender-Specific Strategies

Acknowledge the female SLT epidemic. Deploy grassroots campaigns focusing on the dangers of Zarda and Sadapata tailored for rural demographics.

3. Strict SHS Enforcement

Enforce existing smoke-free laws in public transit, restaurants, and workplaces with measurable penalties to protect non-smokers.

Aligning national policy with WHO FCTC guidelines (SDG Target 3.a)

Conclusion & Limitations

Study Limitations

  • Cross-Sectional: Snapshot of 2017; cannot establish definitive temporal causality.
  • Self-Reporting Bias: Potential underreporting, particularly among women due to cultural stigma.

The Verdict

Bangladesh faces a bifurcated tobacco epidemic. Combustible smoking dominates among men, while smokeless tobacco heavily impacts women and lower-educated rural populations.

Fulfilling WHO FCTC obligations requires moving beyond generalized anti-smoking campaigns toward targeted, culturally aware, and aggressively taxed policies for all tobacco modalities.
