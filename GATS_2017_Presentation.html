Data Preprocessing (Pandas)

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# 1. Map demographic codes into analytical labels
df['Gender'] = df['A01'].map({1: 'Male', 2: 'Female'})
df['Residence'] = df['RESIDENCE'].map({1: 'Urban', 2: 'Rural'})

# 2. Bin continuous age variables into defined demographic cohorts
bins = [14, 24, 34, 44, 54, 64, 100]
labels = ['15-24', '25-34', '35-44', '45-54', '55-64', '65+']
df['Age_Group'] = pd.cut(df['AGE'], bins=bins, labels=labels, right=True)

# 3. Vectorized conditional logic for tobacco modality categorization
conditions = [
    (df['B01'].isin([1, 2])) & (df['C01'].isin([1, 2])),  # Dual User
    (df['B01'].isin([1, 2])) & (~df['C01'].isin([1, 2])), # Smoker Only
    (~df['B01'].isin([1, 2])) & (df['C01'].isin([1, 2]))  # Smokeless Only
]
choices = ['Dual User', 'Smoker Only', 'Smokeless Only']
df['Tobacco_Type'] = np.select(conditions, choices, default='Non-User')

Overall Tobacco Use

Tobacco Prevalence

  • ~35.3% Total Use: 17.3% smokeless, 14.7% smokers, 3.3% dual users.
  • Smokeless Leads: Smokeless tobacco is more common than smoking.
  • Weighting Impact: Raw counts overestimate tobacco use (40.1% raw vs. 35.3% weighted).

Tobacco Habits by Gender

A Clear Divide

  • Men: About 40% smoke cigarettes or bidis.
  • Women: Less than 2% smoke, but about 25% use smokeless tobacco.
  • Current anti-smoking campaigns often miss female users.

When Do Smokers Start?

Starting Age for Smoking

  • Most daily smokers start around age 18.
  • Older adults started at varied ages; younger people mostly start in late teens.
  • Schools are the best place for prevention.

Tobacco Use by Age

How Age Affects Habits

  • Smoking probability rises slowly with age (reaching ~40%).
  • Smokeless tobacco use increases steadily as people get older.
  • Older adults have the highest smokeless tobacco usage.

Tobacco Use by Education Level

Education Lowers Tobacco Use

  • Higher education means much lower tobacco use.
  • People with no formal education use the most smokeless tobacco.
  • University graduates prefer cigarettes over smokeless products.

Daily Cigarette Consumption

Cigarettes Smoked Per Day

  • Smokers average 15 to 20 cigarettes daily.
  • People aged 45-54 smoke the most heavily.
  • Urban smokers consume slightly more than rural smokers.

Urban vs. Rural Tobacco Use

Where People Live Matters

  • Rural areas have much higher smokeless tobacco use.
  • Urban areas have a slightly higher share of cigarette smoking.
  • Cheap bidis and zarda drive high rural usage.

Secondhand Smoke Exposure

Where Are People Exposed?

  • Public transport and restaurants have high exposure despite bans.
  • Many adults still face smoke at their workplaces.
  • Smoke inside homes puts women and children at risk.

Variables Correlation

How Variables Connect

  • Men smoking at home drives high domestic SHS exposure (r = 0.75).
  • Higher education is linked to lower smokeless tobacco use.
  • Smoking and smokeless habits do not usually overlap.

Public Awareness & Knowledge Gaps

What Do People Know?

  • 96.7% Active Awareness: Almost everyone knows smoking causes illness.
  • Only 54.1% SHS Awareness: 42.6% fewer people know secondhand smoke is dangerous.
  • Critical Knowledge Gap: Explains high rates of smoking in homes and vehicles.

Quitting Tobacco

Lack of Medical Support

  • Nearly half of tobacco users want to quit.
  • Doctors rarely advise patients to quit during checkups.
  • Without proper medical support, many people fail to quit.

Policy Recommendations

1. Raise Taxes

Raise taxes on smokeless tobacco and bidis to match cigarettes.

2. Target Women

Run anti-smokeless campaigns specifically for women and rural areas.

3. Enforce Fines

Strictly fine smoking in public transport and restaurants.

Aligning with WHO FCTC guidelines (SDG Target 3.a)

Conclusion & Limitations

Data Limitations

  • This survey is from 2017 (a snapshot in time).
  • People might underreport their habits due to social stigma.

Final Conclusion

Bangladesh has two separate tobacco problems: smoking for men and smokeless for women.

We need targeted solutions for both to improve public health.
